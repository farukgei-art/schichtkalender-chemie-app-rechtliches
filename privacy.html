No data collection by the app

Schichtkalender Chemie runs only on your device. The app has no server, no user account, no advertising and no tracking, and it sends no data to us or to third parties – only when you play with phones nearby, which you switch on yourself, does your iPhone show your gamer tag and your shift to devices nearby and on the same network, and the moves go directly to the phones of your fellow players (see below). What you enter is stored only on your device, in the app’s protected storage, which other apps cannot access; with the app lock you protect it further with Face ID, fingerprint or the device passcode. This also applies to the names and shifts of colleagues you add to your team; a backup file you export yourself contains them along with your other entries. Whether the device backs up its data to iCloud or to the Google account is up to you in the system settings; on Android the app is backed up only with end-to-end encryption. You delete single entries in the app, everything at once with “Reset app” or by deleting the app. Fonts and images are built into the app; it loads none of them from the internet.

Phone calendar

Only if you switch it on in the settings does the app write your shifts and whole days off into a calendar of its own on your device. For this it needs access to the device’s calendars; it neither reads nor changes other calendars. Whether the device syncs this calendar with an account, for example iCloud, is up to you in the system settings; we do not receive this data. If you switch it off again, the app deletes this calendar from the device.

Games in Shift Arcade FG

For the games you can set a gamer tag, also for another player on the same device; next to yours stands your shift from the settings. The app stores tags, games in progress, results with the gamer tag and shift of both players, your friends list and the level last chosen for each game only on your device; they are not part of a backup file. “Delete game data” in the settings removes them, and so does “Reset app”. Playing with phones nearby (iPhone only): only once you tap “Become visible” and access to the local network is allowed does your iPhone make your gamer tag and your shift visible to devices nearby and on the same network that look for these games; only the connection you play over is encrypted. Over it the moves go directly from phone to phone, by Wi-Fi or Bluetooth – without the internet and without a server; we receive none of it. With “Become invisible”, in the background and when you switch the games off in the settings, you are not visible.

App Store, Google Play and crash reports

When you download the app, Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) process data in doing so, for example about your account and device, under their own privacy policies. Depending on your device settings, iOS and Android also send diagnostic and usage data, for example about crashes, to Apple or Google. From this, Apple and Google can provide us with crash reports and statistics that do not allow conclusions about you. On the iPhone you control this under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, on Android under “Usage & diagnostics” in the device’s Google settings. The app itself sends nothing in the process.

Test versions via TestFlight

If you test a pre-release version via Apple’s TestFlight, Apple collects crash reports and usage data of the test version and passes them on to us, together with the device model, device identifier, operating system version and device language; the same goes for feedback, crash reports and screenshots that you send in TestFlight yourself – with your comment and details of the device at that moment (such as mobile carrier, time zone, connection type, battery level, free storage and screen resolution). Your name and email address may be linked to them. We use this information only to find and fix errors (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) and do not pass it on. Apple keeps feedback for one year, crash reports and usage data until the errors are fixed.

These web pages

The privacy policy, legal notice and support are also available as web pages on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub Inc. in the USA. When the pages are visited, GitHub stores the visitor’s IP address for security; this is based on our legitimate interest in providing the pages securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). How long GitHub keeps the IP address is up to GitHub; GitHub names no fixed period, the duration depends on the purpose – here security – and on legal obligations. Providing the IP address is required neither by law nor by contract; without it, however, your device cannot load the pages. GitHub is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; the transfer to the USA rests on the adequacy decision of the European Commission (Art. 45 GDPR). The pages themselves set no cookies and load no fonts, scripts or content from other providers.

Contact by email or phone

If you write to us or call, we process your details only to answer your request – for questions about a purchase under Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, otherwise on the basis of our legitimate interest in answering (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Emails to us are kept in our mailbox at Gmail; Google (Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland) processes them in doing so under its own privacy policy. Otherwise we do not pass on your details and delete them as soon as the request is settled and no statutory retention period applies. Your details are voluntary; without them we cannot answer you.

Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing and data portability, and the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example the State Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information of Rhineland-Palatinate (Landesbeauftragter für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit Rheinland-Pfalz).

Objection

Where we process your data on the basis of our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) – for your request, for the information from TestFlight and when these web pages are visited – you may object to this at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21(1) GDPR); a short message is enough.
